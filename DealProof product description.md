# DealProof: product description

Nov 22, 2026 · @rish

## What DealProof is

**DealProof is an AI analyst for investors and acquirers. It finds companies that match what you are looking for, checks the claims behind them against independent evidence, and tells you which ones deserve a closer look and why.**

It also works in the other direction. For a business that is raising money, DealProof finds investors whose track record matches that business, and shows the past investments that make each one relevant.

*Discover opportunities. Verify the evidence. Evaluate with confidence.*

Today this work is spread across databases, registry lookups, seller presentations and an analyst's spreadsheet. DealProof does it as one connected job: from an investment thesis to a screening case a decision-maker can defend. It does the first-pass research that comes before formal due diligence. It does not replace the accountants and lawyers who come after.

### The problem

An analyst screening a company has to search several sources, work out which legal entity they are looking at, read the seller's presentation, check what can be checked, and write it all up. Several things go wrong along the way.

| What goes wrong | What it costs | What DealProof does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company information is scattered | Slow, repeated research | One search and research flow |
| Companies are compared inconsistently | Subjective screening | The same criteria applied to every candidate |
| Growth signals are mistaken for real traction | Overconfident conclusions | Keeps observed signals apart from confirmed results |
| Seller presentations go unchecked | Decisions rest on unverified claims | Checks each claim and shows the evidence |
| Similar names cause mix-ups | The wrong company's or person's record is attached | Confirms identity first, and says so when it cannot |
| Financial information is missing | Valuations built on guesses | Says what is missing instead of filling the gap |
| Reports take days to assemble | Analyst time spent compiling | Produces the screening memo itself |
| Open questions are never followed up | Screening stays incomplete | Lists exactly what to ask the seller for |

## Who it is for

**The first customers are small M&A advisory firms and corporate development teams that screen acquisition targets every month with only a few analysts.**

The person using it is an investment associate or a corporate development analyst. They want to spend less time compiling and be more certain of what they pass upward. Their fears are a wrong fact in a memo, a recommendation they cannot back up, and a confidential document ending up where it should not.

| Also a fit | What they need most |
| --- | --- |
| Private equity funds | Screening established businesses consistently |
| Early-stage venture funds | Finding startups that match a thesis, and checking founders and traction |
| Roll-up operators | Assessing many similar targets against one strategy |
| Family offices | Independent checks on private businesses |
| Search funds | Finding and first-pass screening of acquisition targets |
| Business brokers | Credible, evidence-backed company profiles |

### Businesses raising money

The second kind of user is a small or mid-sized business looking for capital, or its adviser. They do not know which investors are active in their sector and stage, and they waste months approaching the wrong ones. DealProof gives them a shortlist of investors with the evidence for each.

### The first customer, at ContextCon

A mid-sized Indian logistics company that runs warehousing and transport for other businesses. Its CFO office is considering buying or investing in regional logistics, warehousing and supply-chain technology companies. DealProof finds suitable targets, checks what they claim, and prepares an evidence-backed recommendation before the second meeting.

## What it does

**DealProof works in four steps: Discover, Verify, Evaluate, Act. A user can start at the first step with a thesis, or go straight to the second with a company they already have in mind.**

### 1. Discover

**You describe what you are looking for. DealProof returns a shortlist of real companies and explains why each one is on it.**

- **You give it:** an investment thesis in plain words. For example: "privately held Indian warehousing and supply-chain software companies that serve enterprise customers and show recent expansion".
- **It does:** turns the thesis into clear criteria, separating must-haves from preferences. It lets you edit them, searches live company data, removes duplicates, drops companies that fail a must-have, and ranks the rest by how well they fit.
- **You get:** a shortlist with each company's business, location, size and growth estimates, funding history, hiring activity, reasons for inclusion, and what is still unknown. Every item shows where it came from and when it was last updated.

The fit score measures how well a company matches your requirements. It says nothing about expected returns. Hiring and headcount growth are never treated as proof of revenue.

### 1b. Discover investors

**A business describes itself and what it wants to raise. DealProof returns a shortlist of investors who have backed comparable companies.**

- **You give it:** the business's sector, location, stage and size, and the kind of funding sought.
- **It does:** finds comparable companies that have raised money, reads who invested in them and when, and groups those investments by investor. It ranks investors by how many comparable investments they have made and how recently.
- **You get:** a shortlist of investors. Each entry shows the comparable companies they backed, the round and the date, when they last invested in the sector, why they fit, and what is unknown.

The same principles apply here. An investor appears only when a dated funding record supports it. The fit ranking describes an investor's past behaviour. It does not predict whether they will invest. DealProof does not collect investors' personal contact details, and any message to an investor waits for a person's approval.

### 2. Verify

**You pick a company. DealProof investigates it and checks its claims one by one.**

- **You give it:** a company from the shortlist, or a name and website directly. You can also upload the seller's presentation and documents, or type in individual claims.
- **It does:** confirms which legal entity this is. It builds a public profile of the company, then takes each factual claim and looks for independent evidence. It compares the seller's documents with each other. It gathers recent dated events about the company, and places it next to comparable companies.
- **You get:** a claim ledger. Each claim has a status, the exact evidence, the date of that evidence, and what is missing if it could not be settled.

What it checks first: identity, business and industry, funding events, team size and growth, leadership and founders' career history, and dated public events such as launches and expansions. Where reliable records can be reached it also covers registered security interests, directorships, litigation, customer and partnership claims, and financial filings.

### 3. Evaluate

**DealProof turns what it found into a screening assessment you can explain to someone else.**

It keeps five judgments separate and never blends them into one score.

| Assessment | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Opportunity fit | How well does this company match our strategy? |
| Evidence sufficiency | Do the available records support what we need to know? |
| Business assessment | What do observable operating indicators suggest? |
| Financial assessment | What do reliable financial inputs show, under stated assumptions? Only when such inputs exist |
| Screening disposition | What should we do next? |

The disposition is one of four outcomes.

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Prioritise | Strong fit, and the required checks are satisfied within what DealProof covers |
| Investigate further | Attractive, but important evidence is incomplete |
| Deprioritise | A requirement you set has verifiably failed, or the fit is poor on good evidence |
| Cannot assess | Identity or other essential information is too uncertain to judge |

Missing public evidence never leads to "deprioritise" by itself. Next to every fit score DealProof shows how much of the thesis it could assess, for example "fit 82 out of 100, with 70% of the criteria assessable". A company with thin evidence does not look as ready as one with full evidence.

The evaluation adapts to the kind of company. A young startup is judged on thesis fit, founder relevance, team development and funding history. An established business is judged on strategic fit, identity, operating scale, financial performance, commercial position and leadership continuity.

### 4. Act

**DealProof works out what would settle each open question and helps you ask for it.**

It lists the specific documents needed, drafts one consolidated request to the seller, and waits. Nothing is sent until a named person approves it, and it is sent once. When the seller's documents arrive, they attach to the same case. DealProof rechecks the affected claims, updates the assessment, and shows what changed and why.

## What a finished case contains

**A finished case is a document a decision-maker can read in minutes and audit in full.**

- The opportunity and why it is relevant to the thesis.
- The company's confirmed identity and profile.
- Each material claim, its status and its evidence.
- Recent dated events about the company.
- Comparison with similar companies, where a fair comparison exists.
- Financial evaluation, where reliable inputs exist.
- Risks, assumptions and unknowns, stated plainly.
- A recommended next step, with reasons.
- The exact information to request from the seller.
- A record of every source used, so the case can be reproduced.

### What each claim status means

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported | Adequate evidence directly supports the claim |
| Contradicted | Reliable evidence directly conflicts with the precise claim |
| Partially corroborated | Evidence supports part of the claim, not all of it |
| Conflicting evidence | Relevant sources disagree with each other |
| Cannot verify | Available evidence cannot settle the question. DealProof names what would |
| Not checked | The check was not run, for example because a source was unavailable or the budget ran out |

"Cannot verify" is a legitimate answer, and often the correct one. "Not checked" is never shown as a clean result.

## Principles

**A company can be attractive while its claims remain unverified. DealProof keeps those two things apart.**

1. **Every fact has a source and a date.** Nothing comes from the AI's memory. If no record states it, DealProof does not say it.
2. **The right source for the right question.** A company registry is strong on incorporation. An audited statement is strong on past revenue. A customer's own confirmation is strong on a customer relationship. A company's announcement shows that it announced something, not that it happened.
3. **Estimates are labelled as estimates.** Team size drawn from professional profiles is a signal, not a payroll count.
4. **Identity comes first.** DealProof does not attach a record to a company or a person on a matching name alone. When it cannot tell two apart, it says so.
5. **Gaps are stated, not filled.** Missing information stays missing, and it is never counted against a company as poor performance.
6. **Rules decide, not AI confidence.** The same records under the same policy give the same assessment every time.
7. **A person approves anything that leaves.** No message goes to a seller without a named approver.
8. **Confidential documents stay confidential.** Uploaded files are used only for that case. They are not used to train AI, not shared across customers, stripped of personal details that are not needed, and deleted on a set schedule.
9. **New evidence changes the assessment in the open.** The earlier view is kept, and the reason for the change is shown.

### An example

This is an illustration, not a real company. The logistics client is looking at a regional warehousing operator after a first meeting.

| What the seller said | What DealProof reports |
| --- | --- |
| "Incorporated in 2014, based in Pune" | Supported. The company registry record states both |
| "We employ 250 people" | Cannot verify. A profile-based estimate is lower, but warehouse staff rarely have professional profiles. Request a dated payroll summary |
| "Our founder ran operations at a national logistics firm" | Supported. A professional record shows the role and the years |
| "We raised growth funding in 2025" | Supported. A funding record and a dated announcement agree |
| "We opened a new facility in October 2026" | Partially corroborated. A dated announcement describes a planned facility. Nothing found shows it is operating |
| "We have no debt" | Cannot verify. A lender's registered security is on record, which shows security was given, not that money is owed. Request lender statements |

The assessment that follows: a strong fit with the client's strategy, with three of six claims supported and three open. The disposition is "investigate further", with a request list of three documents waiting for the manager's approval.

## What it is not

**DealProof is the screening that happens before formal due diligence. It makes that later work better aimed. It does not replace it.**

- It is not financial, legal or tax due diligence. Those rest on the seller's confidential books and on signed professional opinions.
- It does not predict returns or say whether an investment will succeed.
- It does not value a company. Where financial figures are supplied, it can run stated scenarios, never a verified fair value.
- It does not give legal advice or recommend deal terms.
- It does not judge people. It reports what records show about roles and dates.
- It cannot see what is not on record. Private company financials, tax positions and many customer relationships are not public in India, and DealProof says so case by case.

### How it differs from what exists

| Existing option | What it does well | What DealProof adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company discovery platforms such as Dealroom.co | Finding companies, market maps, signals | Checks the claims behind a company, not only its profile |
| M&A workflow platforms such as DealRoom | Pipelines, document rooms, coordinating a diligence team | Does the first-pass research itself, against outside evidence |
| Company registry reports | A profile of what is on file for a company | Starts from what the seller claimed and reconciles each claim |
| Pitch-deck screening tools | Fast summaries of a presentation | Indian records, a dated source on every finding, and "cannot verify" as a real answer |
| An analyst working by hand | Judgment and context | The same checks every time, in minutes, with the evidence attached |

The difference is the connection: discovery, independent verification and transparent evaluation in one case. DealProof does not compete on how many companies it lists or how many metrics it shows. It competes on the quality of the finished screening job.

## First version, later versions and the final aim

**The first version does the whole job in a simple form. Later versions deepen each step.**

| Stage | What DealProof does |
| --- | --- |
| First version, at ContextCon | Turns a thesis into a shortlist of real companies. Finds investors for a business raising money, from the record of who has funded comparable companies. Confirms a company's identity. Checks claims about team, leadership, funding and dated events. Shows fit and evidence side by side with a clear next step. Prepares the request to the seller and sends it once after approval. Picks up where it left off if interrupted, and records what each case cost |
| Next | Compares the seller's documents with each other. Benchmarks a company against its peers. Compares several targets under one thesis. Re-evaluates when new evidence arrives. Adds registered security interests and litigation where records are reliable |
| Later | Financial scenarios from supplied statements. Continuous monitoring with alerts when something material changes. Tracking a deal through later stages. Connections to document rooms and customer-relationship tools |

At ContextCon, the organizers' request decides which step has to be strongest on the day.

### The final aim

**Every investment or acquisition opportunity that reaches a decision-maker should arrive with its claims already checked and its unknowns already named.**

DealProof aims to become the standard first-pass analyst for mid-market deals in India, and then beyond. The measure of progress is the number of completed, evidence-backed screening cases that customers accept for a real investment decision.

The one requirement that matters most over time: new evidence can change an assessment without hiding what was believed before, or why it changed.
